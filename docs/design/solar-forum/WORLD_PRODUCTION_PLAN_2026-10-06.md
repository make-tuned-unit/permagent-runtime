# World production plan — 6 October 2026

Owner: planning and independent visual review in the parent task. Implementation: Sol through the task interface. The parent does not write application or asset-generation code.

**Decision:** rebuild the arrival experience and its hero assets around the supplied image. Preserve the real agent integration. Develop a small, complete playable garden first, with Unreal as the leading candidate for the eventual high-fidelity native renderer. Select the shipping renderer only after the same scene has passed visual, performance, interaction, and packaging comparisons on the target Mac. The current large procedural campus is useful source material, but its size and polygon count are not evidence of quality.

This document separates researched capabilities, proposed production decisions, and measured local evidence. “AAA” is an art and usability target, not a verified status or a promise that a generation model produces a finished game.

**User clarification, 6 October:** the landscape must be fully walkable. It can be smaller than the reference but must be equally rich in detail. This overrides any prior plan for inaccessible decorative landscape or visual-only upper terraces. Build a compact finite habitat with connected routes to every apparent destination. Limit the initial finished landscape to what can be fully authored, walked and tested. Celestial sky objects can remain sky scenery; modeled land, gardens, terraces, bridges and buildings presented as destinations require real access. Static splat scenery must not stand in for any traversable part of this landscape.

The novel production approach worth adopting now is a **controlled AI asset loop**: define a consistent design family → generate or author candidate assets → inspect them from multiple views under neutral light → repair in Blender → export optimized runtime assets → compare the actual interactive scene. AI supplies variation and accelerates production; the authored scene and its evidence decide what ships.

The next useful experiment is deliberately small: compare one robot shell, one furnished planter and one table/instrument assembly against the current Blender versions. Freeze dimensions, front/side/back references and material intent. If hosted access is available, test TRELLIS.2 or current HY-3D 3.1; do not pay to generate the whole world before these samples pass. For 3.1, check the selected endpoint rather than assuming every advertised feature is combinable: the documented legacy API disables its LowPoly parameter with model 3.1. [Endpoint specification](https://cloud.tencent.com/document/product/1804/123447) Record quality after cleanup, not merely the first output.

For terrain, use an explicit scene specification with connected elevations, entrances, water boundaries and furnishing zones. Render the same authored layout from arrival, reverse view and each destination. A reviewing agent can identify floating contact, repetitive vegetation, occluded routes and inconsistent scale; Sol repairs the source and the parent verifies the actual runtime. This combines the strongest new research direction with deterministic walking and persistent agent state.

## Art direction: the first 20 metres

Build a compact garden arrival terrace, approximately 18 × 24 metres, with a clearly navigable loop. These are design starting points to validate in play, not a demand to model a vast city first.

Expand the accepted terrace into an approximately 60–90 metre habitat, adjusting extent to quality and performance. Give every apparent destination a route: garden terraces, pavilion interiors, waterfall overlook, lower water path, archive balcony and return bridge. Use stairs plus an accessible alternative where practical; continuous collision and a route graph cover the whole landscape. Landscape size is negotiable, full walkability and detail are not. A decorative distant island is omitted until it can be reached and explored. Foreground quality must hold on reverse views and at each route endpoint.

Allow exploration across the habitat's usable ground, not only a scripted camera track or narrow predetermined tour. Solid objects and water can have clear physical boundaries. Ground that visually appears usable must support the player; every level needs a return route. Beyond route tests, audit connected walking-surface coverage, slopes, step transitions, body/head clearance, edge handling and recovery in the actual game. A flat four-destination prototype does not satisfy this final landscape requirement.

1. **Arrival composition.** Start near human eye height, with a visible robot host 2–4 metres away, a social table, a planted overhead frame and an open route toward the overlook. Use an eye-level perspective rather than a surveillance view. Preserve an overview command for orientation. Keep the existing app navigation familiar.
2. **Three depth layers.** Foreground: ceramic shells, fabric cushions, timber, stone, leaves and small instruments. Middle: garden steps, water and occupied terraces. Far: the reachable upper garden and a planted structural arch, with celestial atmosphere beyond the bounded habitat. Express grandeur through framing and overlap within a compact walkable landscape. Any expanded landscape must join the tested route network.
3. **Hero robots.** One polished base rig, with three readable silhouette variants first. Rounded shells, articulated joints, dark reflective faceplates and expressive restrained eye shapes. Build contact, head tracking, idle, walk and turn motions. Real identity and activity remain authoritative; ambient behavior never invents task progress.
4. **Vegetation.** At least five readable forms: canopy tree, smaller broadleaf shrub, fern-like groundcover, climbing vine and flowering accent. Create branches and thin varied foliage with plausible roots, planters and shadows. Use clustered species distribution and empty walk corridors. Avoid repeating spherical crowns or scattering equal-sized props uniformly.
5. **Architecture and furnishing.** Rounded edge highlights, sensible thickness, joints, drainage, rails and planting troughs. Frame two distinct spaces: an intimate worktable and an open water overlook. Use a small material family across detailed reusable modules. Select a few hero objects rather than filling every square metre.
6. **Lighting.** Warm key and cool fill, soft grounded contacts, readable faces and foliage in shadow. Night should retain tactile detail with warm practical pools. Audit tone mapping, emissive values, exposure and bloom independently. The globe should reveal information instead of washing out everything behind it. Day/night follows the app's current behavior unless a clear World-only lighting preference is added.
7. **Water and atmosphere.** Layered normal motion, shoreline/contact treatment and restrained reflection. Thin waterfall geometry, foam and mist must survive movement and distance. Use atmosphere for depth while preserving contrast at interactable objects.
8. **Environmental life.** Wind varies subtly by plant scale; water and a few distant moving silhouettes create life. Introduce spatial ambience with an obvious mute control and user-started audio. Reduced motion removes unnecessary camera movement and strong ambient motion.

## Asset pipeline and ownership

Sol's asset worker owns a separate bounded output directory and generation scripts. Sol's integration worker owns the production branch and runtime. They must not edit the same files or launch heavy Blender/Unreal jobs concurrently.

For each asset, preserve source file, version, prompt/reference provenance when generated, license/source, dimensions, pivot, material names, collision proxy, texture resolution, triangle count, rig/clip names and export hash. Do not bake lighting into albedo for objects that need relighting. Use consistent metre scale, appropriate normal-map conventions and explicitly verified transparency.

Blender is the editable source of truth for the hero kit. Retopology and rig cleanup are part of the job. Export LODs or equivalent efficient representations; keep repetition instanced. Blender's documentation explains why instances reduce duplicated geometry storage; exporters and runtimes must be checked to ensure that advantage survives. [Blender instances](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/instances.html)

AI experiment acceptance: three asset classes, equal target size, neutral front/back/side/turntable review, one warm and one cool relight, close-up texture review, collision and export checks. Record generation cost and hands-on cleanup time. Reject hidden-side collapse, baked highlights, broken joints, transparent halos and incoherent style. Prefer the method that creates an accepted asset most efficiently; do not choose by novelty alone.

The first procedural foliage review already exposed sparse crowns and repeated flat leaves. Add a small botanical reference comparison before further procedural expansion. Poly Haven provides CC0 assets; inspect lower-resolution payloads and actual topology rather than downloading the full advertised bundle. Candidate examples are [Potted Plant 01](https://polyhaven.com/a/potted_plant_01) for leaf/branch construction and [Potted Plant 04](https://polyhaven.com/a/potted_plant_04) for a small detailed hero prop. Asset rights and service/API terms are separate; preserve provenance. [Poly Haven asset license](https://polyhaven.com/license)

## Renderer decision and deployment

Unreal is the leading high-fidelity candidate because the desired finish benefits from integrated lighting, terrain/foliage authoring and gameplay tools. This is an engineering recommendation, subject to the same-scene benchmark. A renderer swap without new assets would preserve most of the current visible weaknesses.

On macOS, Epic currently documents software Lumen on M1+, Nanite/virtual shadow maps on M2+ as beta, and hardware Lumen/MegaLights on M2+ as experimental. It lists 16 GB memory minimum and 32 GB recommended. The M4 is eligible for several features, but that is not a frame-rate guarantee. Start with supported conservative settings and measure before enabling experimental ones. [Epic macOS requirements](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/macos-development-requirements-for-unreal-engine)

Run the same terrace in the existing app renderer and in Unreal at identical camera, approximate lighting intent and output resolution. Evaluate visual stability as well as a still frame. Epic's Lumen guide describes quality and internal-resolution tradeoffs; its console targets are not measurements on this Mac. [Lumen performance guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine?lang=en-US)

If Unreal wins and meets delivery gates, use a packaged native World runtime launched and supervised by Permagent, with a deliberate return path. Validate real macOS window/focus behavior; do not assume Unreal can be embedded in a WebView. Reuse the authenticated local daemon contract and keep approvals in the existing trusted app interface until full parity is established. Only the necessary workspace state crosses the bridge. Verify reconnect, cancellation, state ordering and process teardown.

A staged build must launch without either editor, the source checkout, development servers or absolute developer-machine asset paths. Test offline behavior, missing assets, fallback, version matching, and clean shutdown. A successful Unreal import or editor screenshot does not prove this. Packaging is an explicit production milestone. [Epic packaging](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/packaging-your-project)

## Playability and learning

The first meaningful loop takes about five minutes: arrive → meet a real agent → inspect a short example → make a prediction or choice → see evidence and explanation → save or revisit a field note. Traversal is optional for ordinary productivity functions.

| Place | Player action | Learning outcome | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival table | Select the host and compare a plan with its constraints | Understand what the agent can do and how a good brief changes the plan | Explain which missing constraint changes the proposed work |
| Garden archive | Inspect two claims and open their sources | Distinguish evidence, inference and uncertainty | Choose the supported claim and receive explanatory feedback |
| Water workshop | Change a bounded illustrative setting, predict an effect, then inspect the result | Understand a simple feedback loop and tradeoff | Compare prediction with the visible model and its stated assumptions |

Educational examples must be clearly labeled demonstrations and must never submit a real task, trade or approval implicitly. Cite the evidence behind factual lessons. The environmental simulation is a teaching model; do not imply real-world scientific accuracy without validation.

Short retrieval prompts and corrective feedback have a stronger educational rationale than passive labels alone. Space optional revisits and allow the user to inspect explanations immediately. This is a design application of the IES learning guide, not evidence that this particular World has learning efficacy yet. [IES practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/PracticeGuide/1)

Provide keyboard-only navigation, readable focus, click-to-select alternatives, clear interaction distance, recoverable collisions, pause, motion sensitivity options, subtitles for speech, and an accessible list equivalent for important spatial actions. Test Escape, modal focus, returning to the app, disconnection and empty/error states. Validate WASD/arrow behavior in the actual runtime rather than just documenting it.

## Proposed budgets and quality gates

All numbers below are initial engineering targets. They are not measured achievements or universal hardware promises.

| Dimension | First acceptance target |
| --- | --- |
| Fast default | Aim for 60 fps at a controlled 1440 × 900 output; p95 frame time ≤ 20 ms after warmup on the target M4 under documented load |
| High-detail fallback | Stable 30 fps tier; p95 ≤ 33.3 ms; record p99 and hitches over 50/100 ms |
| Startup | Interactive arrival within 5 seconds cold on local SSD, within 2 seconds warm; app controls remain responsive during loading |
| Memory | Initial runtime increment target ≤ 2 GB unified memory; report whole-system pressure and swap delta, not just JS heap |
| Asset scope | Hero kit ≤ 40 MB compressed initially; selective 2K materials, occasional justified 4K hero texture; track decoded GPU footprint |
| Motion | No recurrent shimmer, black/white rectangles, geometry popping, face ghosting or obvious transparency sorting along the test route |
| Hidden state | Stop continuous World rendering when hidden; verify reduced-motion behavior separately |
| Play loop | Five-minute route completes without becoming stuck or needing a hidden keyboard shortcut; all real work state remains truthful |
| Full walkability | Every landscape destination has a tested route; verify all terraces, stairs/ramps, interiors, bridge ends, water edges and return paths, with recovery from falls and no visual-only inaccessible land |
| Delivery | Reproducible staged app/runtime launch and return; no editor or development-server dependency |
| Visual quality | Every main camera shows authored depth, plausible materials, varied vegetation, readable agents and controlled light; score alone cannot pass |

Use a deterministic 90-second camera/interaction path with at least two repetitions after warmup. Capture p50/p95/p99 CPU/GPU or frame cadence with the measurement method identified. Record render resolution, pixel ratio/upscaling, quality tier, app/build and asset hashes, time of day, thermal/load context, startup time and memory. Do not equate RAF cadence with GPU time or substitute average FPS for frame-time tails.

Audit draw calls explicitly. Cloning objects while sharing their geometry does not by itself batch their draw calls. Repeated static planting and props need actual renderer instancing or equivalent batching; dynamic robots need sensible material counts, LOD and culling. Start with a deliberately composed social group at arrival and preserve access to all real agent identities through the desk. Do not crowd every roster member into the same small terrace merely to show that their models exist.
