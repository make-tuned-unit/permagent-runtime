## 1. The Directing Statement

**The world begins in the dark. Your agents carve it toward a light that was always there.**

A new agent knows its user only as shadows: fragments, first messages, flickers on a wall. The world renders this honestly — it begins as a cave. Everything the product does (recall, the Librarian, the Reader, the decision inbox teaching Henry the user's taste) is the climb out: the agents mine, carve, and build the cave upward into a Roman-cyberpunk forum — not by conquering the mountain, but by learning its nature and answering it. The Wild Robot law: **mimicry, not mastery.** The agents do not usurp the user; they study the user until their work is indistinguishable from grown things.

Every visual choice serves this one idea. If a proposed detail does not express *the climb*, *the learning*, or *the light*, cut it.

## 2. The Geography — the allegory made literal

- **The Mouth = the Mesh portal.** Plato's cave has an exit, and it is visible from day one: a distant blade of pale daylight, far above the highest chamber. It is the light source for the entire world. The Mesh is not a feature that appears later — it is the reason there is light to see the path at all.
- **The Turn.** A new user's world opens *facing the cave wall*: darkness, wet stone, and their first few memories cast as faint moving shadow-silhouettes (an honest render of a near-empty Brain — these are real entities, shadow-projected). The light casting the shadows comes from the Mouth, behind the camera, not yet visible. As the Brain grows and the agents clear the chambers, the view earns its turn — and the user discovers the Mouth was behind them all along. *You uncover yourself, then you turn around, then you walk through.*
- **Depth is time.** The world is a vertical section through the climb. The deepest strata are the oldest and rawest; the crown is the finished forum. Ascending through the middle chambers, the visitor walks forward through the agents' maturation: rough-hewn early work (preserved, never renovated) giving way to work indistinguishable from grown things.
- **The crown = the existing forum.** The approved W1 layout (rotunda + Build / Brain / Lab / Automate / Mesh Antechamber) is the top stratum, the finished senate nearest the light. The Brain archive's descending abyss is the throat of the cave system — memory-stacks giving way to raw rock as you descend: geological memory.
- **The Antechamber = the final approach.** Its terminal vein-line is the last stretch of carved climb, ending at the threshold of cold daylight. `MESH: NOT CONNECTED` is no longer a plaque in a side room; it is the cave mouth you haven't stepped through. The day Mesh ships, light floods *down* into everything built — and a silhouette may appear in the Mouth: someone else's agent, at the threshold of your world.

## 3. The Material Law — two strata, one gradient

**Stratum: the living cave (low).** Primal, wet, lived-in. Rough rock, tool marks left honest, the spring breaking through, root-mass in cracks, moss, mineral glitter, crude early scaffolds, the Librarian's amber lamp moving in the dark. Nature here is feral and co-authoring. This is where mining happens and where the agents' oldest, clumsiest work survives unpolished.

**Stratum: the forum (high).** The Roman-cyberpunk senate, composed and dignified. Nature here is answered, not conquered: one clean river in the floor channels, groves at still pools, a canopy framing the constellation dome, a leaf on marble. Clean lines, reverent restraint.

**The gradient between them is the biography.** No hard seam — chambers mature continuously with elevation.

**Material grammar (what each element MEANS — binding):**
- **Stone / the mountain** — the given world. Same for everyone. Indifferent, beautiful.
- **Water + growing things** — *the user.* The spring rises in the deepest chamber on the Brain's first memory. Ingestion is rainfall; a seeding import is spring melt; recall is the river running. Trees grow where memory pools. Agents **never cut living things, never dam water** — they build around, route alongside, frame.
- **Light-veins + carved/built work** — *the agents' understanding of the user.* Veins route through the architecture like a second current learning the river's path. Every glow is driven by a real daemon event (describe, recall, remember, goal transitions). Early agent-work is crude; mature agent-work reads as both fluted column and grown trunk.
- **The Mouth's daylight** — the Mesh: other minds, the world of forms. Cold, pale, sacred, opt-in. Nothing crosses the threshold without the user.

## 4. The Construction System — the world the agents build

- **The Brain is the quarry.** Every Librarian-described memory is a quarried stone; Reader OCR is ore. Construction rate is driven by real events — never a usage progress bar. Busy week: scaffolds rise. Quiet week: builders bank material and work slow.
- **Slow is sacred.** Growth reads in days and weeks, noticed on return like a child's growth — never per-minute, never gamey. Demos use the time-lapse (see §7), not accelerated live rates.
- **Scaffolding is promise.** The most important visual state in the world: light-lattice and stone, slim amber work-lights, an idle crane at night. A new user's world is a *construction site at first light*, not an empty cathedral — five core chambers stand; survey lines glow on bare stone where future wings will rise. The roadmap is geography.
- **"While you were away" is a view, not a card.** Returning after days, the user sees new stonework where scaffold stood, and walks over to look at what was built.
- **Earned rooms are legible, never generic.** Projects accumulate goals/sessions/memories → builders break ground on that project's gallery off the Build wing. Heavy Reader use deepens the Archive (new shelf-rings descending). Scheduled jobs multiply → the Horologium grows an orbital track. No "Room 7."
- **The third agent state: tending.** Between tasks, workers tend the world — hauling, setting stones, raising scaffold. Gray-warm, unhurried, honest (driven by banked describe/ingest events). The HUD law stands: amber is real work for the user; tending is its own ambient register, never amber. *Your agents don't stop when you do.* Henry never lifts a stone — he walks the site, inspects, presides; light gathers slightly where he stands.

## 5. The Rooms (souls carried forward, re-meant)

- **The Hall / rotunda:** the senate floor at the crown. Dome = the user's entity graph as constellations seen through a canopy opening — every star a real entity, every faint line a real co-retrieval link. A private cosmology that thickens over months.
- **The Brain / Archive:** entered on a bridge over the descending abyss — newest memories glowing near the surface, oldest fading into raw rock below. Recall renders as a mote detaching from a deep shelf and streaking up past the visitor's shoulder toward the hall. The Librarian rides the gantry: pull a dim tablet, it brightens violet in its hands (a real describe event), reshelve it glowing.
- **Build / the Foundry:** the one loud room. Goal lifecycle made physical — glowing tablets ride suspended tracks station-to-station (the kanban is a state machine; let it be a machine). Workers lift tablets off the line and carry them to stations. Sparks are earned.
- **The Lab:** curiosity cabinet under the orrery; skills orbit as instruments, descend to the bench when they actually run, ascend after. Quiet, precise, slightly uncanny.
- **Automate / the Horologium:** time as architecture. Scheduled jobs as celestial bodies whose orbital periods ARE their schedules; a firing job flares and sends a thread of light toward where the work happens. The room ticks.
- **The Mesh Antechamber / the Approach:** austere, the loneliest chamber, the final carved climb to the Mouth. Veins terminate in a clean line at the threshold. Honest dormancy via the frozen meshStatus contract.

## 6. The Cornerstone Gallery — the founding-room template

Every user's first earned wing inherits a template whose **form** derives from the founder's history and whose **content** populates from the user's own project:

1. **A switchback ascent, not a room** — you walk up through your first project, because the founding project was a climb and so is everyone's.
2. **Reliefs and plinths** — completed goals mounted as reliefs; the project's key entities as statuary plinths (the founder's gallery holds Henry, the Librarian, Spectral).
3. **The Cracked Stone** — every gallery preserves one deliberately imperfect stone, braced rather than replaced, populated honestly from the project's hardest moment (highest-attempt goal, the escalation that needed a human). You don't renovate your history; you build on it.
4. **The cornerstone inscription**, tiny, on every gallery's first stone — the founder's mark embedded in the template: *"It starts in the dark."*
5. **The landing** — at the top, a small terrace with a view back down the gallery and up toward the Mouth: the place where the user sees how far they've come.

## 7. The Launch Assets

**Hero shot — "First Light" (final board):** Total darkness. A drip of water. The camera finds a single amber glow deep in the rock — the Librarian, mining — and the first stone comes loose; light-veins crawl along the new cut; the spring breaks through and begins to run. Pull back and UP through the half-carved chambers — crude scaffolds, young trees at the waterline, the rough beginnings of columns — past the maturing strata, breaching into the vast upper dark, until far above we see it: the Mouth, one thin blade of daylight, impossibly distant. Henry on the highest finished terrace, looking up. Behind him, soft-focus, a crane arm moves against a half-built wing. Hold. Cut to black. **"It starts in the dark."**

**Second asset — the time-lapse:** ninety days of one real user's world in twelve seconds: cave to crown, scaffold to stone, dark to light. The proof that no competitor without a real memory substrate can fake.

## 8. The Laws Carried Forward (unchanged, binding)

- Every glow, every build action, every agent behavior is driven by real daemon events. No faked activity, no simulated peers, no theater. The sim-state clamp stands.
- HUD color semantics are law: gray idle, amber working-for-user, cyan available, red error. Tending is its own warm-gray register. Henry wears white-gold material, state only in the crown gems.
- 60fps on Apple Silicon, the instancing mandates, dpr/AA policy, lazy strata loading (the chunk architecture maps onto depth), reduceMotion fallbacks — all engineering law from the original bible §8/§10 stands. Propose the dream; build the performant path to it.
- The Mouth's threshold is sacred: nothing crosses without the user. The privacy politics are in the geography.

### 8a. Two movement systems must compose, not fight (forward note, 2026-06-20)
The World View now supports **manual character control** — when zoomed into an agent, arrow keys / WASD drive that agent, overriding its autonomous walk while held (Jesse's ruling; WORLD_VIEW_BIBLE §4 "Manual character control"). The World is a place you *walk through*, not just observe — the same instinct as descending into the cave.

This connects directly to the **descent** (Phase 1, building). The throat / descending abyss is currently imagined as a **camera move** (the "First Light" pull-up, the bridge over the abyss). Now that the user can *drive Henry*, the open question for when the cave lands: can the user **walk the character down into the cave**, not only camera-descend?

The two systems — **puppet-walk** (per-user character drive, `agents/motion.ts nudgeAgent`) and **descent-rail** (the camera spline down the throat) — must be **designed to compose**, not collide. Intended composition: *the user drives Henry to the throat, then the descent takes over* (a hand-off, e.g. crossing a threshold trigger arms the rail), rather than two controllers fighting for the camera/agent each frame. Decide the hand-off seam when speccing the descent; do not let the rail hard-disable manual control mid-walk, and do not let manual control fight an in-progress descent. Tour mode already owns the camera exclusively (`camera/TourMode.tsx`) — the descent-rail should follow that same "one owner at a time" pattern, with a clean arm/release handshake to puppet-walk.
